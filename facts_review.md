## A. The knight's tour (29)

- [x] **A1.** Around the 1300s, the Indian poet Vedanta Desika wrote two verses of 32 letters each. Read the first verse in knight's-tour order on a 4×8 board, and you get the second!
  Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knight%27s_tour
- [x] **A2.** About 400 years ago, the Indian scholar Bhat Nilakantha wrote down closed tours that look the same when you turn the board. That was long before Euler.
  Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knight%27s_tour
- [x] **A3.** A tour by the mathematician Abraham de Moivre, printed in 1725, runs around the edges first and fills the middle last. Try it: edge squares are the hardest to reach later!
  Source: https://mayhematics.com/t/1b.htm
- [x] **A4.** The first book to draw knight's tours as lines, instead of numbering the squares, was printed in Bologna, Italy, in 1766.
  Source: https://mayhematics.com/t/1b.htm
- [x] **A5.** In 1771, the mathematician Vandermonde made a knight's tour in 3D, through all 64 cells of a 4×4×4 cube.
  Source: https://mayhematics.com/t/1b.htm
- [x] **A6.** Euler joined four copies of a 5×5 tour into one tour of a 10×10 board, with the same pattern in all four quarters.
  Source: https://mayhematics.com/t/1b.htm
- [x] **A7.** Peter Mark Roget, who made the famous word book Roget's Thesaurus, also wrote about knight's tours. In 1840 he published a quick way to make one.
  Source: https://mayhematics.com/t/1c.htm
- [x] **A8.** The first knight's tour whose rows and columns all add up to the same number was made by William Beverley in 1847.
  Source: https://mayhematics.com/t/1d.htm
- [x] **A9.** The oldest known tour like that on a board bigger than 8×8 is a 12×12 tour. It is credited to the Rajah (king) of Mysore in India, in the 1800s.
  Source: https://mayhematics.com/t/1d.htm
- [x] **A10.** A tour where the rows, the columns and both long diagonals all add up to the same number can't be done on 8×8. The first ones were found in 1962, on boards 16×16 and bigger.
  Source: https://mayhematics.com/t/1d.htm
- [x] **A11.** In 2003, Awani Kumar from India found the first 12×12 tours where rows, columns and both diagonals all add up to the same number.
  Source: https://mayhematics.com/t/1d.htm
- [x] **A12.** In 1917, puzzle writer Henry Dudeney asked for an 8×8 tour where every row, column and both diagonals add up to 260. He thought it couldn't be done. Computers proved him right in 2003.
  Source: https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/16713/pg16713.txt (puzzle 412)
- [x] **A13.** One of the oldest chess puzzles, from 1512, uses a 3×3 board: two white and two black knights in the corners must swap places. It takes at least 16 moves.
  Source: https://proofwiki.org/wiki/Guarini%27s_Problem
- [x] **A14.** On a 3×3 board, a knight can never reach the middle square. The other 8 squares make one big loop, which is why our 3×3 board has a hole in the middle.
  Source: https://proofwiki.org/wiki/Guarini%27s_Problem
- [x] **A15.** The 4×4 board has no knight's tour, but the smaller 3×4 board does. There are 16 of them, counting each direction.
  Source: https://oeis.org/A306282 and Dudeney 1917
- [x] **A16.** Henry Dudeney put a chessboard on each of the 6 faces of a cube. A knight can tour all 384 squares and land back where it began.
  Source: https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/16713/pg16713.txt (puzzle 340)
- [x] **A17.** The 5×5 board has 1,728 knight's tours, counting each direction.
  Source: https://oeis.org/A165134
- [x] **A18.** The 6×6 board has 6,637,920 knight's tours. The 7×7 board has 165,575,218,320!
  Source: https://oeis.org/A165134
- [x] **A19.** The 6×6 board has 9,862 closed tours. They were first counted in 1964.
  Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knight%27s_tour, https://mayhematics.com/t/1i.htm
- [x] **A20.** In 1991, Allen Schwenk found exactly which rectangle boards have a closed tour. For example, 3×4, 3×6 and 3×8 have none, but 3×10 does!
  Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knight%27s_tour
- [x] **A21.** A knight's tour is a famous example of a "Hamiltonian path": a route that visits every point exactly once. It is named after the Irish mathematician William Rowan Hamilton.
  Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knight%27s_tour
- [x] **A22.** Warnsdorff's trick finds a tour on most boards, but it can get stuck on boards bigger than 76×76.
  Source: https://mathworld.wolfram.com/KnightGraph.html
- [x] **A23.** Computers can find knight's tours with a "neural network", a program loosely modeled on the brain.
  Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knight%27s_tour
- [x] **A24.** For a giant board, a computer can cut it into small pieces, find a tour on each, and join them together. This is called "divide and conquer".
  Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knight%27s_tour
- [x] **A25.** The French writer Georges Perec pictured a Paris apartment building as a 10×10 grid. A knight's tour chose the order of the chapters of his 1978 novel.
  Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_A_User%27s_Manual
- [x] **A26.** Chess master George Koltanowski did knight's tours on stage without looking. The audience wrote words in the squares, and he called out each one as the knight landed there. At 80, he did 192 squares at once!
  Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Koltanowski
- [x] **A27.** Over 100 years ago, the English chess master Blackburne often ended his blindfold chess shows with a knight's tour.
  Source: https://mayhematics.com/t/1i.htm
- [x] **A28.** In a 2010 World Championship game, Viswanathan Anand made 13 knight moves in a row. Fans joked that he was trying to do a knight's tour.
  Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knight%27s_tour
- [x] **A29.** Mathematicians even study knight's tours on boards with 4, 5 or more dimensions, which nobody can draw!
  Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/1202.5548

## B. The knight (31)

- [x] **B1.** The knight is the only chess piece that can jump over other pieces.
  Source: https://www.chess.com/terms/chess-knight
- [x] **B2.** At the very start of a game, only knights and pawns can move. Every other piece is boxed in!
  Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knight_(chess)
- [x] **B3.** White has 20 possible first moves: 16 pawn moves and 4 knight moves.
  Source: https://www.chessworld.net/how-many-legal-first-moves-are-there-in-chess.asp
- [x] **B4.** Knight to f3 is the third most played first move in chess, after the pawn moves e4 and d4.
  Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zukertort_Opening
- [x] **B5.** In chess notation the knight is written N, because K was already taken by the king. Old books wrote Kt.
  Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Descriptive_notation
- [x] **B6.** In German the knight is called Springer, which means "jumper". That's why chess puzzle makers often write it as S.
  Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knight_(chess), https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nightrider_(chess)
- [x] **B7.** In Greek the knight is called ίππος (horse), and many people just say άλογο. Its letter is Ι, and its move has the shape of the letter Γ.
  Source: https://el.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ίππος_(σκάκι)
- [x] **B8.** In many languages the knight is simply a horse: caballo in Spanish, cavallo in Italian, kon' in Russian, at in Turkish.
  Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knight_(chess)
- [x] **B9.** In Hungarian the knight can be called a huszár, a horse soldier. In Sicilian slang it's a donkey!
  Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knight_(chess)
- [x] **B10.** A knight in the middle of the board can jump to 8 squares, but a knight in a corner has only 2.
  Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knight_(chess)
- [x] **B11.** Chess players say "A knight on the rim is dim", because a knight on the edge reaches fewer squares. Nobody knows who first said it.
  Source: https://barrypopik.com/blog/a_knight_on_the_rim_is_dim_chess_adage
- [x] **B12.** The French say "Cavalier au bord, cavalier mort": a knight on the edge is a dead knight.
  Source: https://blog.duolingo.com/fr/le-cavalier-aux-echecs/
- [x] **B13.** On an empty 8×8 board, a knight can reach any square in at most 6 moves. Only corner to opposite corner needs all 6.
  Source: https://chessprogramming.org/Knight-Distance
- [x] **B14.** A knight in the corner needs 4 moves to reach the square diagonally next to it!
  Source: https://chessprogramming.org/Knight-Distance
- [x] **B15.** A knight is worth about 3 pawns, the same as a bishop.
  Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knight_(chess)
- [x] **B16.** Knights are strong in crowded positions, because they can hop over the jam while other pieces get stuck.
  Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knight_(chess)
- [x] **B17.** When a knight attacks two pieces at once, it's called a fork. A fork of the king and queen together is called a "family fork" or "royal fork".
  Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fork_(chess)
- [x] **B18.** A knight can attack a queen without being attacked back, because the queen can't move like a knight.
  Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fork_(chess)
- [x] **B19.** In a "smothered mate", the king is boxed in by its own pieces and a knight jumps in to checkmate it. It was already in a chess book from 1497.
  Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smothered_mate
- [x] **B20.** In the Légal Trap, from Paris in the 1700s, White gives away the queen on purpose and checkmates on move 7.
  Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%A9gal_Trap
- [x] **B21.** A king and two knights can't force checkmate against a lone king. The other player has to make a mistake.
  Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two_knights_endgame
- [x] **B22.** King, bishop and knight can checkmate a lone king, but it can take 33 moves and it's tricky. Even grandmasters have failed to do it in real games!
  Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishop_and_knight_checkmate
- [x] **B23.** A pawn that reaches the other side doesn't have to become a queen. In the Lasker Trap, Black wins by turning a pawn into a knight on move 7!
  Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lasker_Trap
- [x] **B24.** The knight's move is the oldest in chess. It has stayed the same for about 1,500 years, while other pieces changed.
  Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knight_(chess)
- [x] **B25.** In Chinese chess the horse moves almost like our knight, but it can't jump. A piece right next to it blocks it, which is called "hobbling the horse's leg".
  Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xiangqi
- [x] **B26.** In Japanese chess (shogi) the knight can only jump forward, so it has just 2 possible moves.
  Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shogi
- [x] **B27.** The knight is thought to be the hardest chess piece to make. In Thai chess sets, it's the biggest piece and is carved by hand.
  Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knight_(chess)
- [x] **B28.** The horse-head knight in most chess sets comes from the Staunton design of 1849. It is thought to be copied from the horses of the Parthenon in Athens.
  Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Staunton_chess_set, https://museum.fide.com/exhibits/staunton-chess-set
- [x] **B29.** World champion Emanuel Lasker gave beginners a rule: bring your knights out before both of your bishops.
  Source: https://chess960frc.blogspot.com/2010/05/knights-before-bishops.html (quotes Lasker's Manual of Chess)
- [x] **B30.** A knight planted deep in enemy land, protected by a pawn, can be worth about as much as a rook!
  Source: https://www.chess.com/terms/outpost-chess
- [x] **B31.** World champion Capablanca played a bigger chess with two extra pieces: the archbishop (bishop + knight) and the chancellor (rook + knight).
  Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capablanca_chess

## C. Chess history (41)

- [x] **C1.** Chess grew out of an Indian game called chaturanga, first clearly mentioned in the 6th century.
  Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaturanga
- [x] **C2.** Chaturanga means "four parts", after the four parts of an old army: elephants, chariots, horsemen and foot soldiers. The knight comes from the horsemen.
  Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaturanga
- [x] **C3.** The first chessboards in India had 64 squares, all of one color. The checkered pattern came later, in Europe.
  Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaturanga, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chessboard
- [x] **C4.** "Checkmate" comes from the Persian "shāh māt", often translated as "the king is helpless".
  Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Checkmate
- [x] **C5.** The rook's name comes from the Persian word "rukh", which means chariot.
  Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rook_(chess)
- [x] **C6.** Long ago the queen was weak: she could move only one square diagonally. Around 1500 in Spain she got her powerful move, and people called the new game "Mad Queen Chess".
  Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_chess
- [x] **C7.** The bishop used to be an elephant! In Arabic the piece is still called "fil", which means elephant.
  Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chess_piece
- [x] **C8.** Castling is the only move in chess where two pieces move at once. Today's rule was settled in France in 1620.
  Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castling
- [x] **C9.** Pawns couldn't always move two squares on their first move. That rule was added later to speed games up.
  Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/En_passant
- [x] **C10.** "En passant" is French for "in passing". "J'adoube" means "I adjust": you say it before you straighten a piece, so nobody thinks you have to move it.
  Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/En_passant, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Touch-move_rule
- [x] **C11.** Touch-move rule: if you touch one of your pieces on purpose, you must move it if you can.
  Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Touch-move_rule
- [x] **C12.** In 1831, 78 chess pieces about 850 years old were found on the Isle of Lewis in Scotland. They're carved from walrus ivory, and some rooks are warriors biting their shields!
  Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewis_chessmen
- [x] **C13.** The board always has a light square in each player's right-hand corner. Remember: "light on the right".
  Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chessboard
- [x] **C14.** White hasn't always moved first! In the 1800s players could pick. One of the first written rules that White starts is from 1880.
  Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_and_Black_in_chess
- [x] **C15.** Moving first is a small advantage: White scores about 52 to 56 percent.
  Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_and_Black_in_chess
- [x] **C16.** Stalemate is a draw. Long ago, some places counted it as a win for the player who caused it!
  Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stalemate
- [x] **C17.** After 50 moves with no capture and no pawn move, a player can claim a draw. This rule first appeared in a book by Ruy López in 1561.
  Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fifty-move_rule
- [x] **C18.** ⏳ ⚠ The longest tournament game ever was played in Belgrade in 1989: 269 moves, more than 20 hours, and a draw!
  Source: https://www.chess.com/blog/ThummimS/the-longest-tournament-chess-game
  ⚠ The source is a blog post; the helper couldn't find the same line on Wikipedia.
- [x] **C19.** The quickest possible checkmate takes just two moves each. It's called "Fool's Mate".
  Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_world_records_in_chess
- [x] **C20.** After just one move each, there are already 400 possible positions. There are more possible chess games than atoms in the universe!
  Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shannon_number
- [x] **C21.** The first international chess tournament was in London in 1851. Adolf Anderssen of Germany won it.
  Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_1851_chess_tournament
- [x] **C22.** Chess clocks were first used at a London tournament in 1883. When time runs out, a little "flag" on the clock falls.
  Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chess_clock
- [x] **C23.** Bobby Fischer invented a chess clock that adds a little time after every move. Most tournaments use his idea today.
  Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chess_clock
- [x] **C24.** Wilhelm Steinitz became the first official World Chess Champion in 1886. Emanuel Lasker held the title longest: 27 years!
  Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Chess_Championship
- [x] **C25.** ⏳ In 2024, Gukesh Dommaraju of India became the youngest undisputed world champion ever, at 18.
  Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gukesh_Dommaraju
- [x] **C26.** Vera Menchik was the first Women's World Chess Champion, from 1927 to 1944. Strong men she beat were jokingly called the "Vera Menchik Club".
  Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vera_Menchik
- [x] **C27.** ⏳ The Grandmaster title was first given in 1950, to 27 players. Today there are about 2,000 grandmasters.
  Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grandmaster_(chess)
- [x] **C28.** ⏳ The youngest grandmaster ever is Abhimanyu Mishra of the USA, at 12 years old in 2021.
  Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_youngest_grandmasters
- [x] **C29.** ⏳ In 2024, 8-year-old Ashwath Kaushik beat a grandmaster in a tournament game. The oldest known player to do it was 82!
  Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_world_records_in_chess
- [x] **C30.** Judit Polgár of Hungary is seen as the strongest female player of all time. At 15 she became the youngest grandmaster up to then, beating Bobby Fischer's record.
  Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judit_Polg%C3%A1r
- [x] **C31.** ⏳ Magnus Carlsen became a grandmaster at 13. His rating of 2,882 in 2014 is the highest ever.
  Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnus_Carlsen
- [x] **C32.** At the 1988 Chess Olympiad, Hungary's teenage team won the women's event with all three Polgár sisters. Judit was only 12!
  Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/28th_Chess_Olympiad
- [x] **C33.** The Chess Olympiad is a team event for countries, held every two years. Greece has played in it since 1950.
  Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chess_Olympiad, https://el.wikipedia.org/wiki/Σκάκι
- [x] **C34.** The Greek word σκάκι comes from the Italian "scacchi". That word goes back to the Persian "shāh", meaning king.
  Source: https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/σκάκι
- [x] **C35.** In Greek, the bishop is αξιωματικός (officer) and the rook is πύργος (tower).
  Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chess_piece
- [x] **C36.** In the Middle Ages, Greek speakers played an older form of chess called ζατρίκιον (zatrikion).
  Source: https://el.wikipedia.org/wiki/Σκάκι
- [x] **C37.** FIDE, the World Chess Federation, was founded in Paris on July 20, 1924. That's why World Chess Day is July 20. Its motto "Gens una sumus" means "We are one family".
  Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIDE, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Chess_Day
- [x] **C38.** In 1770 a "chess robot" called the Turk amazed Europe and played Napoleon and Benjamin Franklin. A person was hidden inside!
  Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanical_Turk
  ⚠ The About screen already tells the Turk's knight's-tour story; this one is about its games.
- [x] **C39.** In 1948, Alan Turing helped write one of the first chess programs. No computer could run it yet, so he worked out its moves by hand, up to 30 minutes per move.
  Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turochamp
- [x] **C40.** In 1997, IBM's computer Deep Blue beat world champion Garry Kasparov in a match, 3½ to 2½.
  Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_Blue_versus_Garry_Kasparov
- [x] **C41.** In 2017, a program called AlphaZero learned chess only from the rules, by playing against itself. After four hours it was stronger than the best chess programs.
  Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AlphaZero
